# TASK 4 – Comparative Study of Bioinformatics Databases

## Objective

To conduct a comparative study of three major bioinformatics databases: **NCBI, UniProt, and Protein Data Bank (PDB)**, and understand their data types, applications, features, advantages, and limitations.

## Databases Studied

### 1. NCBI

**NCBI – National Center for Biotechnology Information**

NCBI provides access to a wide range of biological and biomedical resources, including nucleotide and protein sequences, genes, genomes, literature, genetic variation, and bioinformatics tools.

### 2. UniProt

**UniProt – Universal Protein Resource**

UniProt is a major resource for protein sequence and functional information. UniProtKB contains reviewed and unreviewed protein records, including manually curated Swiss-Prot entries and computationally annotated TrEMBL entries.

### 3. PDB

**PDB – Protein Data Bank**

The Protein Data Bank is a major repository of three-dimensional structural information for biological macromolecules such as proteins, DNA, and RNA.

## Comparative Study

The three databases have different primary purposes:

* **NCBI** – Broad biological and biomedical information
* **UniProt** – Protein sequence and functional annotation
* **PDB** – Three-dimensional molecular structure

These databases are complementary and can be used together in bioinformatics research.

## Methodology

The following steps were followed:

1. Studied the purpose and scope of NCBI, UniProt, and PDB.
2. Explored the databases and their major resources.
3. Compared the type of biological data provided by each database.
4. Compared their applications, advantages, and limitations.
5. Documented the findings in a comparative report.

## Conclusion

NCBI, UniProt, and PDB are important resources in bioinformatics, but each has a different primary focus. NCBI provides broad access to biological and biomedical data, UniProt specializes in protein sequence and functional annotation, while PDB focuses on three-dimensional structures of biological macromolecules. Using these databases together provides a more complete understanding of genes, proteins, their functions, and structures.
